# Quazi-Pharma Inventory Management Rules

## Safety Stock Levels

| Category | Safety Stock Guideline |
|----------|----------------------|
| High-demand OTC (Napa, ACE, Sergel) | 50–100 strips |
| Antibiotics | 25–50 strips |
| Chronic disease (cardiac, diabetes) | 25–30 strips |
| Vitamins & supplements | 25–60 strips |
| Inhalers & nasal sprays | 8–10 units |
| Syrups & liquids | 15–20 bottles |
| Grocery staples (rice, oil, dal) | 10–20 units |
| Personal care (soap, shampoo) | 10–15 units |
| Low-volume items (eye drops) | 8–10 units |

## Expiry Date Management (FEFO)

Pharmacy follows **FEFO** (First Expiry, First Out) — not FIFO.

- All items are arranged with nearest-expiry at the front of the shelf.
- Items within 3 months of expiry: flag for priority sale or return to distributor.
- Items within 1 month of expiry: remove from sale shelf, arrange return/write-off.
- Never sell expired items under any circumstance.

## Reorder Rules

- When stock hits safety level: create Purchase Order to preferred distributor.
- Reorder quantity = 2x safety stock.
- Lead times: Square/Beximco = 1-2 days, Renata/others = 2-3 days, FMCG = 3-5 days.

## Cold Chain Items

Items requiring refrigeration (2-8°C): Insulin, some eye drops, certain syrups.
These are stored in the pharmacy refrigerator, NOT on regular shelves.
Temperature log checked twice daily (morning + evening).

## Controlled Substances

Controlled medicines (sleeping pills, certain painkillers) require:
- Valid prescription from a registered physician
- Entry in the controlled substance register
- Cannot be sold without prescription under any circumstance
